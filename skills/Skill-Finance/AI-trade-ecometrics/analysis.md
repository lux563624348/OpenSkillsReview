# How Will AI Trading Agents Transform Financial Markets?

Before this year’s Spring Festival, I had discussions with some old friends overseas and in Hong Kong—former hedge fund colleagues. A common theme emerged: in the age of artificial intelligence, especially within FICC (Fixed Income, Currencies, and Commodities) trading, will we see a new wave of transformation in financial markets?

This question is essentially a subset of a broader phenomenon: how technological advances in productivity reshape industries and their underlying structures. Here, the focus is specifically on finance—what changes will AI bring?

Since late last year, I’ve been working on my second book, *Witnessing Interconnectedness*, expected to be published by the end of this year. The first chapter revisits the rise of FICC, particularly how information technology—semiconductors and computers in the 1970s and 80s—reshaped financial market structures. These technologies enabled cross-asset linkages and gave rise to entirely new investment strategies.

When discussing AI today, it naturally brings to mind how computing once transformed markets. The key question becomes: how will AI-driven productivity fundamentally reshape financial markets, including tools, strategies, structures, and participants?

---

## The Era of FICC + Crypto

Over the past 5–6 years, traditional FICC assets have increasingly integrated with crypto (C). This has strengthened correlations and made crypto a key indicator of market liquidity. We are now effectively in the **FICC + C era**.

Today, analyzing global liquidity requires observing high-valuation, high-liquidity crypto assets like Bitcoin.

However, the focus here is not asset classes—but **AI Trading Agents** and how they may reshape investor structures, especially affecting small institutions and younger professionals.

---

## Historical Parallel: The Computer Revolution

In the 1980s, technologies such as PCs, spreadsheets, Bloomberg terminals, and Reuters systems expanded computational and quantitative capabilities. They enabled:

- Cross-asset modeling  
- Correlation analysis (rates, FX, commodities)  
- Macro hedge strategies and arbitrage  

Markets transformed in three key ways:

1. **Speed**: From minutes to seconds → birth of programmatic trading  
2. **Information Processing**: Exponential growth → explosion of derivatives  
3. **Institutional Advantage**: Hardware + algorithms → dominance over retail investors  

Manual trading methods (paper orders, phone calls, floor shouting) were replaced by system-driven trading. Markets evolved from fragmented/manual to systematic/interconnected.

By 2000:
- Institutional ownership exceeded 70%  
- Retail participation (direct) fell below 30%  
- Markets became more efficient and less emotional  

This was not the disappearance of retail investors, but a structural shift toward professionalization.

## AI Competition: Rapid Stratification

Today mirrors the 70s–80s:

- Productivity advances  
- Structural reorganization  
- Institutional evolution  

AI’s core application in finance is **AI Trading Agents**, now increasingly widespread.

If market participants adopt AI and you don’t, your relative efficiency declines.

### Retail Investor Divergence

Retail investors are splitting into two groups:

1. **AI-enabled investors**
   - Use AI for research, modeling, execution  
   - Rapidly approaching small institutional capability  

2. **Non-AI users**
   - Rely on social media and intuition  
   - Falling behind quickly  

AI Trading Agents replicate the path of programmatic trading—but at exponentially greater speed and scale.

Key facts:
- AI now accounts for over 90% of global trading volume  
- Algorithmic trading exceeds 70% in U.S. equities  

---

## Democratization of Financial Capability

Open-source AI trading tools are expanding rapidly.

Retail investors can now:
- Conduct deep research via natural language  
- Monitor multi-asset markets in real time  
- Automate risk management  
- Execute trades end-to-end  

This significantly reduces:
- Cost  
- Complexity  
- Information asymmetry  

Previously, tools like Bloomberg or Reuters cost tens of thousands annually. Now, AI provides similar capabilities at near-zero cost.

---

## The Rise of “Mini Hedge Funds”

A new phenomenon may emerge:

- 1–3 person hedge fund-like operations  
- Individuals leveraging AI to operate at institutional levels  
- Platforms enabling compliance and affiliation  

Large institutions still maintain advantages in:
- Computing power  
- High-frequency infrastructure  

But AI is “retailizing” professional tools.

---

## Impact on Institutions

### Small & Mid-Sized Firms

Face major challenges:

- Previous edge over retail investors is disappearing  
- Must either:
  - Invest heavily in data, models, infrastructure  
  - Transition to advisory/service roles  

The middle ground is shrinking.

### Large Institutions

Continue to dominate via:
- High-frequency trading  
- Advanced infrastructure  
- Proprietary data  

They are pushing toward:
- Millisecond → microsecond → nanosecond execution speeds  

---

## Workforce Transformation

AI will reshape financial jobs:

### Most at Risk
- Data entry  
- Report writing  
- Basic modeling  
- Compliance checks  

### Emerging Role of Traders
- From execution → supervision of AI  
- Strategy design and system oversight  

A senior PM may now manage what once required an entire team.

---

## Who Will Not Be Replaced?

AI equalizes **efficiency**, but not **true alpha generation**.

Those least likely to be replaced:

- Individuals with strong frameworks and methodologies  
- Strategic thinkers with philosophical depth  
- Decision-makers under uncertainty  

AI cannot yet:
- Build original frameworks  
- Understand regime shifts  
- Navigate black swan events  

Experienced investors retain advantages:
- Crisis experience  
- Pattern recognition  
- Macro intuition  

AI excels at:
- Pattern recognition in known environments  
- Probabilistic optimization  

But struggles with:
- Structural breaks  
- Geopolitical shocks  
- Game-theoretic complexity  

---

## A Bifurcated Future

The industry is moving toward polarization:

- **Top tier**: Strategy + AI integration  
- **Bottom tier**: Low-skill, replaceable roles  
- **Middle layer**: Most vulnerable  

---

## Final Perspective: Efficiency vs Fairness

Technological progress prioritizes **efficiency**, not equality.

Regulation should aim to:
- Prevent extreme distortions  
- Ensure access to tools  
- Avoid exploitation of loopholes  

But absolute fairness is impossible.

---

## Historical Reminder

Past technological shifts brought risks:

- 1987 crash  
- LTCM collapse (1998)  
- Quant crises (2007–2008)  

Yet these are not reasons to halt progress.

The real challenge is:
- Managing risk during innovation  
- Balancing efficiency and fairness  

---

## Conclusion

AI Trading Agents are not just a tool upgrade—they represent:

- A restructuring of market participants  
- A redistribution of capabilities  
- A redefinition of competitive advantage  

Those who adapt will thrive. Those who don’t will be left behind.
